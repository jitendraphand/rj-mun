Signal type

Pure tones give a band-by-band answer. Pink noise is broadband — good for a quick "listen to the difference" demonstration, but the meter cannot file it under one band.

Automatic sweep

Plays every selected band in turn, with a short silence between them, so the baseline run and the panel run are identical. Start the sweep after arming the Sound Meter on the other device.

Keep the media volume fixed The on-screen level control and the phone's own volume buttons multiply together. Set the phone volume once, before the baseline run, and do not touch it — including not letting the screen lock and re-unlock into a different volume. If the volume changes between the two runs, the insertion loss you calculate is the volume change, not the panel.
Hearing safety Sustained pure tones are tiring. Keep the level moderate, never hold the speaker close to an ear, and stop immediately if a tone feels uncomfortable.

Why these six frequencies?

125 Hz, 250 Hz, 500 Hz, 1 kHz, 2 kHz and 4 kHz are the standard octave bands used to rate building materials. Each is double the one before, and together they cover the range that carries speech, traffic and machinery noise.

What "dBFS" means

The level control is in decibels relative to full scale — the loudest signal the device can produce without distorting. −12 dBFS is a quarter of full amplitude, which leaves plenty of headroom so the speaker stays clean.

Using a Bluetooth speaker

Phones are weak below 250 Hz. Connect a small speaker and this page drives it exactly the same way — the low-frequency results become far more trustworthy. Pair the speaker before starting, and never re-pair mid-run.
